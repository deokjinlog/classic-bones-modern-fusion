An X-ray of 112,936 real story plots

Same
Bones

Strip the skin off two stories from different centuries, continents, and genres — and sometimes the very same skeleton stares back.

Originality is mostly recombination. Below are six specimens pulled from a corpus of 112,936 published plots: pairs and trios that look nothing alike and are, underneath, one story wearing different flesh. The differences are skin. The bones are shared.

Plate 01 — succession by fratricide

The Lion King=Hamlet

Match confirmed · 4 shared vertebrae
Shared skeleton
  1. A king is murdered by his own brother, who takes the throne.
  2. The dead king appears as a ghost to his son and names the killer.
  3. The rightful heir, paralysed by guilt, exiles himself from the kingdom.
  4. He returns, forces the usurper to confess, and destroys him.
1994 · animated musical

The Lion King

Pride Rock, a wildebeest stampede, "Hakuna Matata," a father's face in the stars.

c. 1600 · revenge tragedy

Hamlet

Elsinore, poison poured in a sleeping ear, a play within a play, a ghost on the battlements.

Same bones, one true divergence: Hamlet dies of his hesitation; Simba survives his. Tragedy and myth are the identical story told to opposite ends.

Plate 02 — the hollow crown

The Godfather=Macbeth

Match confirmed · 4 shared vertebrae
Shared skeleton
  1. A loyal, capable man is shown an opening to power — and pushed toward it by an intimate and a violent provocation.
  2. He commits one murder to seize what feels owed to him.
  3. Each killing forces the next; old friends become threats to be purged.
  4. He wins the throne and loses his soul, alone behind a closing door.
1972 · crime epic

The Godfather

A war hero drawn back in, a restaurant hit, a christening intercut with a massacre.

c. 1606 · Scottish tragedy

Macbeth

Three witches, a drugged guard, a banquet haunted by Banquo, Birnam Wood on the march.

Only the pusher differs — Lady Macbeth's ambition, or the bullets that put Vito in the street. Both men mistake the crown for safety, and it costs them everyone they love.

Plate 03 — the reluctant chosen one

Star Wars=The Matrix

Match confirmed · monomyth family
Shared skeleton
  1. A nobody senses his ordinary world is a lie he can't name.
  2. A wise older mentor appears, reveals the hidden truth, and calls him "the one."
  3. The mentor is taken — struck down, or captured and given up — so the young man can go on.
  4. Guided by the mentor beyond death, he masters the hidden power and breaks the dark lord.
1977 · space opera

Star Wars

A desert farm boy, Obi-Wan's lightsaber, the Force, a voice in the trench: "let go."

1999 · cyberpunk

The Matrix

A cubicle hacker, Morpheus's red pill, a simulated world, a phone booth back to the sky.

The same skeleton walks through The Lord of the Rings, Harry Potter, and half of myth — Joseph Campbell named it the hero's journey. The desert, the code, the ring are only its costume.

Plate 04 — the creator's reckoning

Frankenstein=Jurassic Park=Blade Runner

Match confirmed · Promethean trio
Shared skeleton
  1. A brilliant maker uses new science to create life that "shouldn't" exist.
  2. Warnings about playing god are waved away in the thrill — or the profit — of it.
  3. The creation slips its maker's control.
  4. It turns on him, and the miracle becomes a judgement.
1818 · gothic novel

Frankenstein

A stitched corpse jolted alive in a watchtower lab; "It's alive!"; a monster who only wanted flowers.

1993 · sci-fi blockbuster

Jurassic Park

Cloned dinosaurs, "life finds a way," the frog DNA that lets the caged thing breed and break free.

1982 · neo-noir

Blade Runner

Replicants built to serve, four years to live, and a creation that comes home to meet its maker.

Only our sympathy moves — we pity the monster, fear the dinosaurs, and root for the replicant. The Promethean bones underneath never change: make life, lose control, answer for it.

Plate 05 — alone against the sea

Cast Away=Life of Pi=Robinson Crusoe

Match confirmed · castaway trio
Shared skeleton
  1. A sudden disaster strands a man alone, cut off from all of society.
  2. He must learn raw survival against an indifferent nature.
  3. His one bond is with a non-human companion he talks to, needs, and grieves.
  4. The ordeal remakes him; he returns to a world he can no longer fully rejoin.
2000 · survival drama

Cast Away

A FedEx man on a Pacific island, and a volleyball named Wilson.

2001 · magical-realist novel

Life of Pi

A boy in a lifeboat sharing it with a Bengal tiger named Richard Parker.

1719 · the first English novel

Robinson Crusoe

A shipwrecked sailor, a footprint in the sand, and a companion named Friday.

Three centuries apart, the same lonely bones: the companion is the whole story. Without something to keep for, survival isn't a plot — it's just weather.

Plate 06 — assemble the crew

Ocean's Eleven=Seven Samurai

Match confirmed · 4 shared vertebrae
Shared skeleton
  1. A leader takes on a job too big for one person, for stakes both practical and personal.
  2. He recruits a crew one by one — each a specialist with a signature skill.
  3. They drill and plan the near-impossible operation.
  4. The execution turns on a hidden gambit; they win, but the team is changed and thinned.
2001 · Las Vegas heist

Ocean's Eleven

Con men in tuxedos cracking a casino vault to take a fortune that isn't theirs.

1954 · Japanese epic

Seven Samurai

Masterless rōnin recruited to defend a starving village that can pay them only in rice.

The aim inverts — one crew takes, the other protects — but recruit-the-specialists-for-one-impossible-job is the same machine. Change the target and it becomes a war film, a caper, or a fellowship.

The point isn't that these stories are unoriginal. It's that the skeleton is the part you're free to keep — so all the craft can go into the skin.

How this was made

Each skeleton was drawn by reading the actual plot summaries in WikiPlots — 112,936 plots scraped from Wikipedia (Mark Riedl, Georgia Tech; CC-BY-SA) — and stripping each story down to the structure it shares with the others. It's the same corpus David Robinson once measured for the average "shape" of a story, but left as static charts.

What this is — and isn't

A hand-curated first slice, not the finished instrument. These six were chosen to be recognisable; the corpus skews toward notable, older, English-language works, and the matches are picked, not yet computed at scale. The full version would embed all 112,936 skeletons and let you drop in any story — or your own logline — and surface its structural twins automatically.
